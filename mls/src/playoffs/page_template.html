Who moves on? Every 2026 playoff tie, forecast two ways

One forecast reads the betting market. The other ignores it, and judges each team only by its profile: how it creates chances, how it concedes them, and the players it has been using. Where the two agree, the pick is solid. Where they split, there is something worth a closer look.

Computed on 16 November 2026, before the wild-card matches on 19 November. This version stays as it is so both forecasts can be checked against the results. Any update will be published alongside it.

Where the forecasts disagree

Both forecasts make {{top_name}} the favourite. Their four disagreements:

  • Chicago. The profile forecast likes Chicago ({{pp_chi}}% to win MLS Cup, against {{p_chi}}% from the market). Chicago's attack is built mainly on passing, the biggest passing contribution of any playoff team. In the East the profile forecast has Chicago ({{pf_chi_f}}%), Nashville ({{pf_nsh_f}}%) and Inter Miami ({{pf_mia_f}}%) almost level for the conference title. The market has Nashville clear on {{mk_nsh_f}}%.
  • San Jose v Houston. The market has this one close (Houston {{mk_hou_sf}}%); the profile forecast makes San Jose a {{pf_sje_sf}}% favourite. Houston's attacking profile is the weakest in the West field.
  • The West's lower half. The profile forecast has FC Dallas narrowly ahead of LAFC and St. Louis for a place in the conference final; the market has LAFC.
  • Nashville. The market has Nashville on {{p_nsh}}% to win it all; the profiles have them on {{pp_nsh}}%. Their profile is built on defence more than attack.

The profile forecast never sees the betting lines, and the market can price news the profiles cannot see, such as injuries. A disagreement is a question to look into, not a sign that either side is wrong. Against the prediction market's title odds, the rank correlation is {{rho_market}} for the market forecast and {{rho_profile}} for the profile forecast.

The profile forecast, and what did not work

The profile forecast predicts each side's expected goals from its profile and its opponent's, using public American Soccer Analysis data from {{pt_matches}} regular-season matches (2017–2025, excluding 2020):

  • How a team creates: goals added (g+) per game through receiving, passing, dribbling and shooting, plus possession.
  • How a team concedes: g+ conceded per game through the same four actions, plus how hard it presses.
  • Who has been playing: minute-weighted ratings of each team's wide attackers, strikers and 10s, fullbacks and centre-backs, using players' minutes in the last ten weeks of the season.

The idea worth testing was that matchups matter: that a team which creates through dribbling does extra damage to a team that leaks chances to dribblers, or that a possession side struggles against a press. Before any model was fitted, seven such matchups were written down in the project log, with a rule. The matchups would be used only if adding them improved predictions in at least 7 of 8 held-out seasons.

The matchup effects were not supported. They improved predictions in {{pt_imp}} of 8 seasons, and on average made them slightly worse. Every one of the seven estimates is close to zero, with an interval that includes zero:

MatchupEffect on xG95% interval

Effect is the change in log expected goals for one standard deviation of both features together. An effect of 0.01 is about a 1% change in expected goals.

So the profile forecast uses each team's own profile only: what a team does matters, but how its style meets the opponent's barely does. The tie-by-tie breakdown above shows where each side's edge comes from; it adds no matchup effect on top. The player-unit ratings also added little beyond the team profiles, so recent changes in who plays barely move this forecast.

Has this worked before?

Both forecasts were run on the 2023, 2024 and 2025 playoffs, the three seasons played under the current format, using each season's regular season only. How it works

Market forecast

Each regular-season match's closing odds (Pinnacle, or the market average where Pinnacle is missing, with the bookmaker margin removed) are turned into an expected number of goals for each side. A least-squares fit then gives every club an attack and a defence rating plus a league-wide home advantage, with recent matches weighted more (90-day half-life). This is the same scoring model as Paper 1 and the same conversion from odds as Paper 2. Home teams score about {{home_mult}} times as many goals as they would on neutral ground. The fit is repeated on {{draws}} resamples of the season's matches, and the probabilities are averaged over them.

Profile forecast

A Poisson regression of each side's match xG on its attacking profile and its opponent's defensive profile, with all features standardised within each season. The 2026 forecast uses the 2026 regular-season profiles. Uncertainty comes from {{draws}} draws of the model's coefficients, and both forecasts use the same bracket engine.

Playoff rules
  • Wild card (8 v 9): one game at the 8 seed. A draw after 90 minutes goes straight to penalties.
  • Round One: best of three, at the higher seed, then the lower seed, then the higher seed. Every drawn game goes to penalties.
  • Conference semifinals and finals: one game at the higher seed, with 30 minutes of extra time, then penalties.
  • MLS Cup: one game hosted by the finalist with the better regular-season record.

Extra time is scored at a third of the 90-minute rate. Penalty shootouts are treated as 50/50.

Exact, then checked by simulation

The bracket probabilities are calculated exactly for each set of ratings. The page's "play it out" engine was then run on 400,000 random brackets, and every probability matched the exact value to within {{js_diff}} percentage points.

Limits
  • Who is available. Neither forecast sees injuries, suspensions or call-ups. Paper 2's absence study found that losing a team's best player costs far more than losing a depth player, so a missing star matters most.
  • Shootouts are treated as coin flips, and every club gets the same home advantage.
  • Built on the market. The market forecast's ratings come from betting lines, so it is calibrated against the market rather than independent of it. The profile forecast is the independent one.
