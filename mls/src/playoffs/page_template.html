Where the model and the market disagree

The model and the prediction market agree on the order of the field (rank correlation {{rho_market}}) and on the same three favourites. The one large gap is Vancouver: {{p_van}}% here against {{pm_van}}% in the market. Nashville ({{p_nsh}}% vs {{pm_nsh}}%) and Inter Miami ({{p_mia}}% vs {{pm_mia}}%) are close.

The model sees only the regular-season betting lines and the shape of the bracket. A market can also price news the model cannot see, such as injuries, suspensions and who is available. A gap is a question to look into, not a sign that either side is wrong.

A second check uses Paper 1's Dixon-Coles model, fitted to 2026 goals instead of betting lines. It changes the top of the order: Nashville {{dc_nsh}}%, Vancouver {{dc_van}}%, Inter Miami {{dc_mia}}%. In the backtest below, ratings from goals predicted playoff matches less well than ratings from betting lines, so this page uses the betting-line ratings.

How it works

Ratings

Each regular-season match's closing odds (Pinnacle, or the market average where Pinnacle is missing, with the bookmaker margin removed) are turned into an expected number of goals for each side. A least-squares fit then gives every club an attack and a defence rating plus a league-wide home advantage, with recent matches weighted more (90-day half-life). This is the same scoring model as Paper 1 and the same conversion from odds as Paper 2. Home teams score about {{home_mult}} times as many goals as they would on neutral ground.

To carry uncertainty in the ratings, the fit is repeated on {{draws}} resamples of the season's matches, and every probability is averaged over them.

Playoff rules
  • Wild card (8 v 9): one game at the 8 seed. A draw after 90 minutes goes straight to penalties.
  • Round One: best of three, at the higher seed, then the lower seed, then the higher seed. Every drawn game goes to penalties.
  • Conference semifinals and finals: one game at the higher seed, with 30 minutes of extra time, then penalties.
  • MLS Cup: one game hosted by the finalist with the better regular-season record.

Extra time is scored at a third of the 90-minute rate. Penalty shootouts are treated as 50/50.

Exact, then checked by simulation

The bracket probabilities are calculated exactly for each set of ratings. The page's "play it out" engine was then run on 400,000 random brackets, and every probability matched the exact value to within {{js_diff}} percentage points.

Limits
  • Who is available. The ratings cannot see injuries, suspensions or call-ups. Paper 2's absence study found that losing a team's best player costs far more than losing a depth player, so a missing star matters most.
  • Shootouts are treated as coin flips, and every club gets the same home advantage.
  • Built on the market. The ratings come from betting lines, so the model is calibrated against the market rather than independent of it.
